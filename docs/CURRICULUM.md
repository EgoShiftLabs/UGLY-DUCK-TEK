# UGLY DUCK TEK — V0.2 Teaching Notes

## The teaching standard

The goal is to turn a user's idea into a more evidence-based and responsible project. The self-guided curriculum is **practice**, not credentialed instruction. A learner can start with a musical release, community program, website, education resource, or another legitimate creative tool. Token ownership is never required.

## Four stages per track

**Study:** Read three substantive sections and write down what the learner previously assumed about the subject.

**Example:** Open the worked example and explain why each step creates evidence instead of adding hype or complexity.

**Build:** Respond to four structured prompts in original language, not copied example text. Each prompt has a length requirement; the student confirms the rubric has been checked. This confirms only that a local draft exists, not that it is good.

**Practice:** Make five decisions in ambiguous situations, then review why the recommended answer is stronger. Retakes are unlimited and retain the best local score. The questions are formative, not proctored.

## Scoring and completion

A track has two independent milestones:

- Knowledge: at least **4 correct out of 5** scenario answers in the browser.
- Practice: **four unique written sections**, at least **30 characters each**, at most **900 characters each**, plus self-review acknowledgement.

The 12 milestones across six tracks drive the progress meter. The complete local practice receipt requires all 12. A partial portfolio export is available after any one saved artifact.

The following rubric questions are reflective rather than independently scored:

| Track | Key evidence a mentor would later examine |
|---|---|
| Orientation | Real audience, actual problem, small deliverable, falsifiable test |
| Literacy | Data-flow model, trust boundary, minimum information, failure case |
| Execution | User outcome, owned tasks, testable acceptance, contingency |
| Governance | Review/execution separation, consent/conflicts, appeal or exception |
| Safety | Protected asset, realistic threat, prevention owner, incident response |
| Long-term | Maintenance cost, contributor rights, succession and orderly closure |

## Instructor or pilot facilitation

For a 45–60 minute group session, use one track:

1. Five minutes: ask learners to state a personal project idea and its audience.
2. Ten minutes: work through one instruction section and surface assumptions.
3. Ten minutes: examine the worked example and its failure pattern.
4. Fifteen minutes: draft the four-part artifact independently.
5. Ten minutes: critique responses with consent and complete the scenario check.
6. Five minutes: ask what evidence learners will collect before the next meeting.

If a learner cannot name a specific deliverable or test, the right intervention is feedback and revision—not a digital certificate.

## Pilot questions

Evaluate with participants who are new to digital project development, not only crypto enthusiasts:

- Can the learner describe the project problem without relying on jargon?
- Can they identify a plausible alternative explanation for their research?
- Are artifact fields genuinely different in content or filled with repeated boilerplate?
- Did they complete the work independently, and where did they get stuck?
- Can they explain one scenario answer in their own words?
- Can a learner using keyboard navigation or assistive technology reach the same tools?

Track comprehension and quality of work, not just completion percentages.
